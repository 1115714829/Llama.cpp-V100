Verdict: your number is real

Measured 222–263 tok/s on this machine. My earlier framing — that the fast numbers belonged to the NVFP4 path and that plain FP8 was the slow one — was wrong for short context. (FP8 is slower at 256K: their README lists FP8 256K no-MTP at 41.11 tok/s and NVFP4 256K at 50.376.)

4. Two explanations already excluded

  • Not quantization bytes. Theirs: FP8 27 GB over 4 cards ≈ 6.75 GB/card. Ours: Q4_K_M 18.5 GB over 3 cards ≈ 6.2 GB/card. Comparable, yet 4× apart.
  • Not CUDA graphs being off. Our build has GGML_CUDA_GRAPHS:BOOL=ON (checked in build/CMakeCache.txt), and the arch gate only disables pre-Volta (ggml-cuda.cu:4404: cc < GGML_CUDA_CC_VOLTA → disabled). V100 is allowed. (Whether the speculation round, whose batch shape changes every round, actually hits a cached graph still needs a separate A/B — tracked as a follow-up.)

5. The structural finding: decode never touches Volta's Tensor Cores

llama.cpp runs its decode matmul as a GEMV, on zero Tensor Cores

Our own ncu profiling already showed decode is ~77% mul_mat_vec_q — a pure GEMV. In llama.cpp the only route to Volta's FP16 Tensor Cores is the mmf/FP16-MMA path at ne11 ≥ 64, i.e. prefill only. Decode (M=1) and the M≈5–8 speculation verification batch all go through GEMV / dp4a.

1cat does the opposite: they run the M≈5 verification GEMM on Volta Tensor Cores — vendored csrc/sm70_turbomind/ (lmdeploy SM70 GEMM, 884 = HMMA m8n8k4 tile), plus a dedicated study of exactly this shape set in docs/design/sm70_awq_exact_m5_batched_gemv.md (5×17408×5120, 5×5120×8704, 5×8192×5120, 5×5120×3072; 236 calls/rank per verification round; accepted operator = TurboMind FP32-accumulating HMMA with CTA_N=32/64, 9.7688 ms, 236/236 bitwise-equal).

The physics: at M=1 a GEMV is purely bandwidth-bound, so Tensor Cores cannot help — reading the weights is the entire cost. At M≈5–8 the arithmetic intensity rises 5–8×, the GEMV stops being bandwidth-only, and whoever can use Tensor Cores wins. That is the physical origin of 222–263 tok/s, and of our 63–91 ms rounds.

6. Correcting the record

My earlier report (phase1a-report.html) concluded "even on a compatible target DFlash2 loses to MTP, so the 120–200 target rests on FP8 which V100 lacks — the goal does not hold on this box." That conclusion is retracted. Both of its load-bearing reasons were wrong:

  • "DFlash2 is slower than MTP" — true only of llama.cpp's unoptimised DFlash2 path. 1cat runs the same DFlash2 idea on the same hardware generation at 222–263 tok/s.
  • "V100 has no FP8, so the premise fails" — not a barrier: 1cat stores FP8 and bridges it to FP16 compute. Verified live on this machine.

What still stands from that report: the port is correct (PR #27858 fixed a real crash, host selector verified); and the production TurboFCFusion Q4_K_M target is genuinely incompatible with the base-model DFlash2 drafter (AL 1.2–1.4) — a separate, target-side fact.

The goal is reachable. Same hardware generation, comparable weight bytes per card, equal acceptance length. The gap is implementation efficiency in the decode round.

7. Levers, in value order

  • 1. Run the M≈5–8 quantized matmul on Volta FP16 Tensor Cores (HMMA m8n8k4). Directly attacks the 4×. Landing zone: mmq.cuh / mmq.cu's Volta dispatch (today ne11 5–63 goes to MMQ with dp4a). Big engineering, V100-specific — exactly this project's mandate.
  • 2. Verify the speculation round actually hits a CUDA graph. Cheap: A/B with GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS=1, plus graph-hit counters. Shape churn may be invalidating the key.
  • 3. GDN fusion (their FUSED_GDN_VERIFY / FlashQLA). Low priority: our GDN is only ~1.4% of kernel time.
  • 4. Draft-side cost — they keep the draft in the graph and quantize the lm_head (VLLM_SM70_DFLASH2_QUANT_LM_HEAD); we read a separate 1.14 GB draft per round.
